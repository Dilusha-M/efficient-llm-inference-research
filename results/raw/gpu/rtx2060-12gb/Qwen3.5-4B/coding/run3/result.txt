### Explanation of Design Choices

#### 1. Why Naive Substring Matching Fails
Naive substring matching (e.g., `"event=decode" in line`) is prone to three specific failures:
*   **Wrong Event Type**: A line like `event=decode_speed` would pass a simple substring check `"event=decode" in line` but fail the strict token check. Conversely, `event=decode tps=10` works, but `event=decode tps=10.5` also works. The danger is distinguishing `event=decode` from `event=decode` appearing as part of another key like `event=decode_and_write`. Tokenization (`split()`) ensures we deal with atomic `key=value` units, preventing partial matches.
*   **Malformed Duplicates**: A naive check might see `event=decode` twice and assume it's fine, or see `event=decode` and `event=write` and assume they are different keys. The requirement treats duplicate keys (same prefix) as malformed regardless of value. Token-based iteration allows us to count occurrences of `event=` and `tps=` prefixes explicitly.
*   **Whitespace Sensitivity**: Without splitting, a line `event= decode` (space between key and value) would be missed by a simple search for `event=decode`. Tokenization normalizes whitespace, ensuring we only look at the exact string `event=decode`.

#### 2. Empty-Result Policy
If `count` remains 0, the dictionary returns `None` for `min_tps`, `max_tps`, and `avg_tps`. This prevents division by zero errors and provides a clear signal that no valid data was found, rather than returning `0.0` for an average which would be semantically incorrect.
